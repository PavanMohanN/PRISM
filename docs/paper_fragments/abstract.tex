\begin{abstract}
Recovering physical parameters from indirect, noisy observations---the ill-posed inverse problem---requires three properties that contemporary learning-based methods provide only in isolation: exact forward--inverse consistency, satisfaction of hard physical constraints, and calibrated posterior uncertainty that faithfully represents multiple competing explanations. Operator surrogates predict the forward map but cannot invert it; invertible networks invert but enforce constraints only through soft penalties; and neural posterior estimators quantify uncertainty without guaranteeing either consistency or feasibility, and frequently collapse genuinely multimodal posteriors onto a single mode. We introduce PRISM, a single liquid time-constant neural ordinary-differential-equation flow that unifies all three properties. Because the flow is a diffeomorphism, reverse-time integration yields an exact inverse whose cycle-consistency error equals the numerical solver tolerance rather than the value of a trained penalty. Fixed projection layers render every recovered solution feasible by construction, so that the constraint-violation rate is provably zero. An observation-conditioned mixture base distribution then amortizes calibrated, multimodal posteriors in a single forward pass. We establish exact reversibility and a Gr\"onwall stability bound on the recovered inverse map, and we validate both results empirically. Across controlled synthetic benchmarks that span partial-differential-equation operator inversion and simulation-based inference, and that are evaluated on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution splits, PRISM matches or surpasses state-of-the-art invertible-network, flow-matching, and neural-posterior baselines on inverse accuracy and calibration, while remaining the only method to attain zero constraint violations together with machine-precision reversibility. We release a reproducible benchmark suite and a scikit-learn-style library.
\end{abstract}
